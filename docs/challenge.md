# Space Apps 2026 challenge: Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map

Source: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/interplanetary-survival-guide-martian-map/ (text pasted by the team on 2026-11-13; the site does not load from machine B because of a certificate error). Difficulty: Intermediate. Subjects: Human Exploration, Mars, Planets & Moons, Software, Space Exploration.

## Summary
Create a layered, integrated view of a location or route on the Martian surface that pulls together data from multiple NASA science missions and could help a human explorer plan and carry out a successful Marswalk while conducting new and exciting science along the way.

## Objectives (quoted)
- Create a map, tool, or interface a Mars astronaut could use during a surface exploration mission, using NASA data and tools.
- "For example, if you chose a science objective of learning more about boxwork formations, a geologic phenomenon the Curiosity rover studied in 2025, you would want to pick a location where the formations are abundant, like the Mount Sharp region where Curiosity observed them."
- "You may consider including at least three kinds of data: optical, thermal, spectral, topographic, geophysical, atmospheric, radiation, and chemical are a few examples."
- Astronaut safety first: "you might include space weather data."
- Spacesuits "possibly carrying scientific equipment and other supplies. Consider how this might impact their mobility and, if possible, include a route map that shows where the astronaut will travel to accomplish their scientific objectives while avoiding significant obstacles such as large rock formations."

## Potential considerations (optional)
- Display formats: inside the spacecraft or habitat, a tablet the crew carries, a helmet-visor HUD.
- Static snapshot, interactive product, or a tool that generates maps of different locations or data types.
- Inspiration from NASA maps of Earth, the Moon, other planets and exoplanets.
- Time frame: past, predicted future, or none.
- Point-of-view images from Mars surface missions, so the astronaut knows what to expect.
- Rovers or robots carrying crew and tools, or astronauts working in pairs.
